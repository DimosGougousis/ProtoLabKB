# Change Management for AI-Augmented Engineering Workforce Transformation

## Purpose

This document defines the comprehensive change management framework for transforming mechanical engineers from technical drafters and designers into **AI-Augmented Client Advisors** — high-value pre-sales specialists who leverage AI-assisted design systems to deliver faster, more accurate, and more profitable client solutions.

The transformation fundamentally redefines the engineering role:
- **Before:** Engineers spend 60-70% of time on drafting, design iteration, and technical specification
- **After:** AI handles drafting and initial design; engineers focus on client advisory, design ownership, quality review, and strategic pricing

This is not simply an IT change — it is an **organizational transformation** that touches identity, incentives, workflows, skills, and client relationships. The change management approach must address the psychological, economic, and operational dimensions simultaneously.

## When to Use

This change management framework applies when:

- Deploying AI-assisted design systems that automate drafting or design generation
- Transitioning engineering teams from execution roles to advisory roles
- Implementing AI-powered pricing or quotation systems
- Restructuring engineering workflows to incorporate AI co-pilots
- Redefining engineer performance metrics from utilization to value creation
- Launching new advisory service offerings enabled by AI capacity release

## 1. Engineer Resistance Taxonomy for AI-Augmented Transformation

### 1.1 The Identity Crisis: From Craftsman to Advisor

The most profound barrier is not technical — it is **identity**. Mechanical engineers derive professional worth from their ability to design, draft, and solve technical problems. Asking them to let AI handle the design work feels like asking a surgeon to let a robot operate while they watch.

**The core psychological threat:** *"If AI does the design, what am I?"*

This is fundamentally different from previous automation waves. CAD replaced drafting boards but engineers still designed. CNC programming replaced manual machining but engineers still programmed. AI-assisted design replaces the **thinking** — and that strikes at the heart of engineering identity.

### 1.2 Resistance Classification Matrix

| Type | Symptoms | Root Cause | Prevalence | Intervention Priority |
|------|----------|------------|------------|----------------------|
| **Identity Threat** | "I didn't become an engineer to be a salesperson." "This devalues my expertise." "AI can't match my judgment." | Professional identity tied to technical execution, not advisory work | Senior engineers (15+ years), domain experts, those with patents/publications | **Critical** — must be addressed first |
| **Skill Anxiety** | "I don't know how to do consultative selling." "I'm not comfortable in client-facing roles." "What if I can't learn this?" | Legitimate skill gap; fear of incompetence in new role | Mid-career engineers, introverts, those with limited client exposure | **High** — requires structured skill building |
| **Economic Fear** | "Will my compensation change?" "Is this a way to reduce headcount?" "What happens if I don't adapt?" | Rational economic calculation; observed precedent of automation reducing jobs | All levels, especially those with financial obligations | **High** — requires structural guarantees |
| **Quality Gatekeeper** | "The AI will make errors I'd catch." "Client safety depends on my review." "I can't sign off on AI-generated designs." | Legitimate concern about quality degradation and liability | Quality engineers, those who have fixed costly errors, safety-critical domain experts | **Critical** — must preserve quality standards |
| **Change Fatigue** | "Another initiative that will fail." "Management doesn't understand our work." "We tried this before." | Erosion of trust; previous failed transformations | Unionized environments, organizations with history of failed digital initiatives | **Medium** — requires trust rebuilding |
| **Comfort Zone** | "I'm good at what I do now." "Why fix what isn't broken?" "I'm too old to change careers." | Loss aversion; preference for known competence over unknown potential | Engineers within 5-10 years of retirement, high performers in current role | **Medium** — requires compelling personal benefit |

## 2. The Transformation Journey: 5-Phase Program

### Phase 0: Foundation (Weeks 1-4) — "Understand Before You Change"

**Objective:** Build psychological safety, establish trust, and create the conditions for transformation.

#### 2.0.1 Leadership Alignment Workshop

Before any communication to engineers, ensure leadership is aligned and credible:

| Activity | Participants | Outcome |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| Business case presentation | C-suite, Engineering Directors | Shared understanding of why transformation is necessary |
| Role evolution mapping | HR, Engineering Directors, Sales Directors | Concrete new role definitions with compensation models |
| "What's in it for them" exercise | Leadership team | Personalized value propositions for each engineer cohort |
| Commitment ceremony | All leaders | Public, written commitment to structural guarantees |

**Critical deliverable:** Written, signed **Transformation Guarantee** that commits to:
- No involuntary layoffs due to AI adoption for [24] months
- All engineers who complete transition receive evolved role with ≥ current compensation
- Engineers who cannot or choose not to transition receive 6-month outplacement support
- Quality review authority preserved — engineers retain final sign-off on all client deliverables

#### 2.0.2 Engineer Listening Tour

Conduct 1:1 confidential interviews with every engineer (30-45 minutes each):

**Interview Protocol:**
1. "What do you love most about your current work?"
2. "What frustrates you most about your current role?"
3. "If you had 40% more time freed up from drafting, what would you want to do with it?"
4. "What concerns you most about AI-assisted design?"
5. "What would make you excited about an advisory role?"
6. "What support would you need to feel confident in a new role?"

**Output:** Individual resistance profiles, skill gap assessments, motivation maps, and cohort segmentation.

#### 2.0.3 Peer Champion Identification

Identify 3-5 engineers who are naturally curious, respected by peers, and open to change:

| Champion Profile | Why They Matter |
|-----------------|----------------|
| **The Skeptical Expert** | Respected senior engineer who is cautiously open; their endorsement carries more weight than any executive communication |
| **The Technical Innovator** | Engineer who already uses AI tools informally; can demonstrate practical value |
| **The Client Whisperer** | Engineer with natural client relationship skills; can model the advisory role |
| **The Rising Star** | High-potential mid-career engineer hungry for growth; sees transformation as career accelerator |

**Champion Treatment:**
- Early access to AI tools (2 weeks before general rollout)
- Direct access to transformation leadership
- Budget for external training (consultative selling, advisory skills)
- Quarterly recognition and bonus for adoption milestones
- Explicit permission to fail and provide honest feedback

### Phase 1: Awareness & Desire (Weeks 5-10) — "Why This Matters to You"

**Objective:** Create compelling awareness of the transformation and build genuine desire to participate.

#### 2.1.1 The "Day in the Life" Experience

Create immersive experiences that show engineers the future state:

**Before/After Simulation Workshop (Half-day):**
- Engineers work through a real client order using current workflow (timed)
- Then work the same order using AI-assisted workflow (timed)
- Calculate time savings and discuss what they'd do with freed capacity
- Introduce the advisory scenario: "The client calls back with a design change. With AI, you respond in 15 minutes instead of 4 hours. What does that do for the relationship?"

**Client Testimonial Videos:**
- Record interviews with pilot clients who experienced the new service model
- Focus on client perspective: "My advisor caught a design optimization I hadn't considered and saved me 15% on tooling costs."
- Let clients make the case for why advisory is more valuable than execution

#### 2.1.2 The "Cobot Can't Do This" Challenge

Adapted from the Change Management Orchestrator's intervention toolkit:

**Implementation:**
- Frame it as a challenge: "We think the AI can handle standard CNC design for aluminum parts. Prove us wrong."
- Engineers submit their hardest edge cases, trickiest tolerances, and most nuanced material selections
- AI attempts each challenge. Results are evaluated by the engineer
- When AI fails, the engineer's correction becomes training data (and earns a bounty)
- When AI succeeds, engineer grudgingly acknowledges competence (and often becomes curious)

**Why It Works:** Engineers are competitive and skeptical. Letting them try to break the system channels resistance into engagement. Every "failure" they find makes the system better AND earns them recognition as the expert who caught it.

#### 2.1.3 Role Evolution Roadmap Presentation

Present concrete, individualized role evolution paths:

| Current Role | Evolved Role | New Responsibilities | Compensation Change | Timeline |
|-------------|-------------|---------------------|-------------------|----------|
| CNC Design Engineer | CNC Solutions Advisor | Client consultation, design review, pricing optimization, vendor negotiation | Base + 15% advisory commission | 6-9 months |
| Injection Molding Engineer | Molding Process Consultant | DFM advisory, material selection consulting, tooling optimization, client education | Base + 20% advisory commission | 6-9 months |
| Sheet Metal Fabricator | Fabrication Solutions Architect | Complex assembly advisory, cost optimization, alternative process recommendation | Base + 18% advisory commission | 9-12 months |
| Quality Engineer | Quality Assurance Director | AI output validation, client quality SLA design, continuous improvement leadership | Base + 12% quality bonus | 6 months |
| Senior Process Engineer | Strategic Manufacturing Consultant | Multi-process advisory, supply chain optimization, innovation partnership | Base + 25% advisory commission | 9-12 months |

**Key message:** "Your compensation goes UP, not down. Your career path accelerates, not stalls. Your expertise becomes MORE valuable, not less — because now you're selling judgment, not hours."

### Phase 2: Knowledge & Ability (Weeks 11-24) — "Learn the New Craft"

**Objective:** Build the specific skills engineers need to excel as client advisors while maintaining their technical authority.

#### 2.2.1 The Advisor Skill Stack

Engineers need a specific set of skills they've never been trained in. The training must be practical, not theoretical.

| Skill Domain | Specific Skills | Training Method | Duration | Certification |
|-------------|----------------|----------------|----------|---------------|
| **Consultative Selling** | Needs discovery, value articulation, objection handling, closing | Role-play workshops with real client scenarios | 3 days intensive + 6 weekly practice sessions | Internal certification |
| **Client Relationship Management** | Active listening, expectation management, proactive communication, trust building | Paired client visits with experienced advisor | 4 client visits over 8 weeks | Client feedback score ≥ 4.5/5 |
| **Technical Advisory** | Design optimization, material selection consulting, DFM review, cost engineering | Case study workshops using real ProtoLabs orders | 2 days + ongoing case reviews | Advisory quality score ≥ 90% |
| **AI Co-Pilot Mastery** | AI tool proficiency, output interpretation, quality validation, override protocols | Hands-on lab with real AI design system | 2 days + 4 weeks supervised practice | AI proficiency certification |
| **Pricing & Margin Optimization** | Value-based pricing, margin analysis, competitive positioning, negotiation | Pricing simulation exercises | 1 day + monthly pricing reviews | Pricing accuracy ≥ 95% |
| **Business Acumen** | P&L understanding, client lifetime value, market positioning, competitive landscape | Monthly business review participation | Ongoing | Business case presentation |

#### 2.2.2 The "Teach-Back" Protocol (Adapted)

Instead of asking engineers to "train the AI," position the transformation as teaching the next generation:

**Implementation:**
- Pair each resistant senior engineer with a junior engineer for structured knowledge transfer
- Junior engineer learns to use AI tools; senior engineer teaches the **judgment** behind the designs
- AI system observes the teaching interaction and extracts decision patterns
- Senior engineer is told: "You're mentoring [junior name]. The AI is learning from watching you teach."
- Junior engineer becomes the AI co-pilot operator; senior engineer becomes the quality reviewer and client advisor

**Why It Works:** Engineers are more willing to teach humans than machines. The human intermediary removes the psychological barrier. The junior engineer becomes an advocate who can demonstrate AI competence to the senior.

#### 2.2.3 Reverse Mentoring with AI Shadowing

**Implementation:**
- Deploy AI design system in **observation-only mode** for 4-6 weeks. No automation. Just watching and logging engineer decisions.
- AI asks clarifying questions via interface: "I noticed you selected 6061-T6 aluminum instead of 7075-T6 for this bracket. The client specified high-strength requirements. Can you tell me why?"
- Engineers answer when convenient. No forced sessions. No time pressure.
- After observation phase, AI presents its "understanding" back to the engineer for correction: "Here's what I think I learned about your material selection logic. What did I get wrong?"

**Why It Works:** Engineers enjoy teaching when positioned as masters, not data sources. The correction step gives them control and validates their expertise.

#### 2.2.4 Micro-Knowledge Bounties

**Implementation:**
- Break AI training into **discrete knowledge units**: "Decision tree for wall thickness optimization in injection molding" or "When to recommend steel vs aluminum for structural brackets."
- Each unit has a bounty value ($100-$1,000 depending on complexity and business impact)
- Engineers claim bounties by submitting knowledge in their preferred format: voice memo, annotated photo, short video, written procedure, or 10-minute conversation with a knowledge engineer
- Bounties are **stackable** — 10+ units in a quarter gets a 1.5x multiplier
- Leaderboard (opt-in) with quarterly recognition and bonus

**Why It Works:** Converts abstract "help the AI" into concrete, bounded, compensated tasks. Respects that knowledge has value.

### Phase 3: Pilot & Reinforcement (Weeks 25-36) — "Prove It Works"

**Objective:** Run controlled pilots that demonstrate the new model works, build confidence, and generate evidence for broader rollout.

#### 2.3.1 Pilot Design

| Pilot Parameter | Specification |
|----------------|--------------|
| **Scope** | 2-3 product lines (e.g., CNC aluminum parts, injection molded prototypes) |
| **Participants** | 4-6 engineers (mix of champions and moderately resistant) |
| **Duration** | 12 weeks |
| **Client Selection** | 10-15 existing clients with repeat order history |
| **Success Metrics** | See 2.3.2 |
| **Governance** | Weekly pilot review with Transformation Sponsor |

#### 2.3.2 Pilot Success Metrics

| Metric | Baseline (Current) | Target (Pilot) | Measurement Method |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Order turnaround time** | 5-8 business days | 2-3 business days | Order management system |
| **Engineer capacity (orders/week)** | 3-5 orders | 12-20 orders | Time tracking system |
| **Client satisfaction (NPS)** | 35-45 | 55-65 | Post-order survey |
| **Average order margin** | 22-28% | 30-38% | Financial system |
| **Design revision rate** | 15-20% | 8-12% | Order management system |
| **Engineer satisfaction** | Baseline survey | +20 points | Quarterly pulse survey |
| **Client advisory interactions** | 0.5 per order | 2.5 per order | CRM system |
| **Revenue per engineer** | $X/month | 2.5X-3X/month | Financial system |

#### 2.3.3 The "Shadow Authority" Model

Give pilot engineers formal authority over the AI system:

**Implementation:**
- Each AI deployment has a named **Human Supervisor** (the pilot engineer)
- Supervisor can override any AI-generated design with a single click
- All overrides are logged and become training data for the next iteration
- Supervisor receives a dashboard showing AI performance, error rates, and improvement trends
- Supervisor has authority to "pause" AI operation if they judge quality is at risk

**Why It Works:** Converts resistance into stewardship. Engineers who can control the AI feel ownership, not threat. The override mechanism also generates the highest-quality training data (expert corrections).

#### 2.3.4 Failure Archaeology Sessions

Instead of asking "how do you design X?", ask "tell me about a time a design failed and you fixed it."

**Implementation:**
- Facilitated sessions (45-60 min) where engineers narrate failure scenarios and their resolution logic
- Structured format: What happened → What you noticed → What you tried → What worked → What you'd do differently
- Knowledge engineers transcribe and structure the narratives into decision trees and exception-handling rules for the AI
- Engineers review the structured output and approve/modify it

**Why It Works:** War stories are ego-affirming. Engineers get to demonstrate expertise through narrative, not through filling out forms. The failure scenarios are also the highest-value knowledge for AI training (edge cases, exception handling).

## 3. AI System Change Management (Technical Layer)

While the human transformation is the primary challenge, the AI systems themselves require rigorous change management. This section governs changes to the AI design systems that engineers depend on.

### 3.1 AI Change Classification

| Classification | Description | Examples | Approval Authority | Lead Time |
|---------------|------------|---------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Standard** | Pre-approved, low-risk, repeatable changes | Scheduled retraining on same data source; monitoring threshold adjustment; prompt template update | AI Engineering Lead | Same day |
| **Normal** | Non-routine changes requiring assessment | New model version; feature set changes; design algorithm updates; pricing model changes | Engineering Director + AI Risk Review | 5 business days |
| **Emergency** | Changes to prevent active harm or critical failure | AI producing unsafe designs; compliance violation; critical performance degradation | Engineering Director (immediate); post-hoc review within 5 days | Immediate |

### 3.5 Impact Assessment for AI Design System Changes

| Impact Dimension | Assessment Questions | Risk Indicator |
|-----------------|---------------------|---------------|
| **Design accuracy** | Will this change affect the accuracy of AI-generated designs? | High if accuracy drops >2% |
| **Client impact** | Will clients experience different design outputs or turnaround times? | High if client-facing behavior changes |
| **Pricing impact** | Does this change affect automated pricing or margin calculations? | High if pricing logic changes |
| **Safety impact** | Could this change introduce unsafe design recommendations? | Critical if safety-critical domains affected |
| **Advisor workflow** | Does this change alter how advisors review and approve AI outputs? | Medium-High if review process changes |
| **Regulatory scope** | Does this change trigger new regulatory obligations? | High if yes |

### 3.6 Impact Scoring

| Score | Definition | Governance Requirement |
|-------|-----------|----------------------|
| **Critical** | Change fundamentally alters AI design behavior or introduces safety risk | Engineering Director + CAB + Board notification |
| **Major** | Change materially affects design accuracy, pricing, or client experience | Engineering Director + AI Risk Review |
| **Moderate** | Change has measurable but contained effect within existing boundaries | AI Engineering Lead approval |
| **Minor** | Change has negligible effect on AI behavior | AI Engineering Lead (log only) |

## 5. Rollback Requirements for AI Design Systems

### 5.1 Mandatory Rollback Capabilities

Every AI design system deployment must have a tested rollback capability. This protects both client deliverables and engineer trust in the system.

| Requirement | Description |
|------------|------------|
| **Previous version preserved** | Prior model version, design algorithms, and pricing logic must be stored and deployable |
| **Rollback procedure documented** | Step-by-step instructions that any AI engineer or advisor can follow |
| **Rollback tested** | Rollback procedure must be tested in staging before production deployment |
| **Rollback time SLA** | Maximum time to complete rollback, defined per system criticality |
| **Rollback trigger criteria** | Specific, measurable conditions that automatically trigger rollback |
| **Advisor notification** | Affected advisors must be notified immediately when rollback occurs |

### 5.2 Rollback Time SLAs

| System Criticality | Maximum Rollback Time | Trigger |
|-------------------|----------------------|---------|
| Client-facing design generation | 15 minutes | Automated (monitoring) or manual (advisor reports issue) |
| Internal pricing engine | 1 hour | Manual decision by AI Engineering Lead |
| Advisory support tools | 4 hours | Manual decision by AI Engineering Lead |

### 5.3 Example: CNC Design AI Rollback

The CNC design AI is updated with a new toolpath optimization algorithm. Post-deployment monitoring detects a 12% increase in design revision rate within 1 hour.

- **Rollback trigger:** design revision rate exceeds 115% of baseline for >30 minutes
- **Automated action:** monitoring system alerts AI Engineering Lead + rolls back to previous algorithm
- **Rollback time:** 10 minutes (within 15-minute SLA)
- **Advisor notification:** affected advisors notified via dashboard alert + email within 15 minutes
- **Post-rollback:** CR-AI updated with rollback evidence; root cause analysis initiated; advisor feedback collected

## 9. Escalation Protocol

When interventions fail or resistance exceeds thresholds:

| Level | Trigger | Action | Owner |
|-------|---------|--------|-------|
| **Level 1** | Single engineer resistance | 1:1 diagnostic conversation; adjust individual support plan | Change Management Lead |
| **Level 2** | Team-level resistance (>30% of team) | Team workshop; structural adjustment to incentives or workflow | Engineering Director |
| **Level 3** | Cross-team resistance or quality incidents | Pause automation in affected domain; executive intervention | Transformation Sponsor |
| **Level 4** | Organization-wide resistance or client impact | Board-level review; fundamental strategy reassessment | CEO/Board |

**Critical principle:** If engineers are actively sabotaging or disengaging, stop AI operation in their domain. Forcing it creates worse resistance. Accept partial adoption — not every domain needs full AI automation immediately.

## 10. Funnel Intake Integration

### 10.1 When Change Readiness Review Triggers

Every use case that passes through the `/pl-funnel-intake` process now includes a **Box 7: Change Readiness & Human Impact** assessment. This section defines how that assessment integrates with the broader change management framework.

**Trigger conditions:**
- Any use case that involves AI-assisted design, drafting, or decision-making
- Any use case that changes engineer workflows, responsibilities, or client interaction patterns
- Any use case that introduces new tools engineers must learn or adopt
- Any use case with resistance risk score >= 16 (Medium or higher)

### 10.2 Funnel-to-Change-Management Handoff

```
Funnel Intake Complete (6-box canvas + Box 7 + appendices)
         |
         v
+--- Box 7 Resistance Risk Score? --------------------------+
|                                                            |
| LOW (6-15)                    MEDIUM+ (16+)                |
| v                             v                            |
| Proceed to                    +--- Trigger Phase 0 -------+|
| Feasibility Probe             | - Listening tour           ||
| (no CM overhead)              | - Champion identification  ||
|                               | - Transformation Guarantee ||
|                               | - Compensation planning    ||
|                               +----------------------------+|
|                                    |                       |
|                                    v                       |
|                               Adjust ROI:                  |
|                               Business Value               |
|                               - Technical Cost             |

|                               - Attrition Risk             |

|                                    |                       |
|                                    v                       |
|                               Proceed to                   |
|                               Feasibility Probe            |
|                               WITH CM plan attached        |
+------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 10.3 Adjusted ROI Calculation

When Box 7 identifies Medium+ resistance risk, the ROI hypothesis from Box 3 must be adjusted:

| Component | Source | Notes |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Gross Business Value** | Box 3 ROI hypothesis | Revenue increase, cost reduction, capacity gain |
| **Technical Implementation Cost** | Appendix B | Engineering weeks, cloud costs, licensing |
| **Change Management Cost** | Box 7.3 | Training, productivity dip, CM program, bounties |
| **Attrition Risk Cost** | Box 7.3 | Replacement cost for at-risk personnel |
| **Adjusted ROI** | Gross Value - Tech Cost - Change Cost - Attrition Risk | **This is the real ROI** |
| **Time to Value** | Technical Build + Change Adoption | **This is the real timeline** |

**Critical rule:** A use case with positive unadjusted ROI but negative adjusted ROI must be redesigned or deferred. Do not proceed with a use case where the human transformation cost exceeds the business value.

### 10.4 Buy-In Strategy Activation

Based on Box 7.5 recommendation, activate the corresponding change management program:

| Box 7 Strategy | Change Management Activation |
|---------------|------------------------------|
| **Champion-Led** | Identify champion from Box 2 RACI; provide early access, training budget, recognition program |
| **Pilot-First** | Design 12-week pilot per Phase 3 of this document; select volunteer team; define success metrics |
| **Executive-Mandated** | Secure Transformation Sponsor commitment; issue Transformation Guarantee; launch full 5-phase program |
| **Redesign-Then-Proceed** | Return to funnel intake with modified use case that reduces human impact; re-evaluate Box 7 |
| **Defer** | Log use case with deferral rationale; schedule readiness reassessment in 6 months |

### 10.5 Continuous Feedback Loop

After use case implementation, feed outcomes back into the funnel intake process:

| Feedback Signal | Source | Action |
|----------------|--------|--------|
| Actual change cost vs. Box 7 estimate | Financial system + HR | Update change cost estimation model |
| Actual resistance vs. Box 7 scoring | Engineer surveys + attrition data | Calibrate resistance risk scoring |
| Actual adoption timeline vs. estimate | LMS + CRM | Adjust Time to Value assumptions |
| Engineer satisfaction post-transformation | Quarterly pulse survey | Update resistance taxonomy weights |

This feedback loop ensures that each subsequent funnel intake produces more accurate change readiness assessments.

### 10.6 Data Engineering Integration with Change Management

Box 8 (Data Readiness & Engineering) identifies data work that often requires domain expert involvement. This creates a dependency between data engineering and change management:

**Data Work Requiring Human Engagement:**

| Data Activity | Human Required | Change Management Implication |
|--------------|---------------|------------------------------|
| **Data labeling** | Domain expert labels training data | Must be compensated (knowledge bounties); must not exceed 20% of expert's time |
| **Data validation** | Expert reviews data quality | Must be positioned as quality stewardship, not clerical work |
| **Rule extraction** | Expert codifies decision rules | Use Failure Archaeology sessions (Phase 3); ego-affirming narrative format |
| **Feature definition** | Expert defines meaningful features | Use Reverse Mentoring (Phase 2); AI asks, expert answers |
| **Edge case identification** | Expert identifies exceptions | Use "Cobot Can't Do This" challenge (Phase 1); channels resistance into engagement |
| **Feedback review** | Expert evaluates model outputs | Use Shadow Authority model (Phase 3); gives expert control |

**Data Readiness Impact on Transformation Timeline:**

| Data Readiness Score | Impact on Change Management |
|---------------------|----------------------------|
| **4.0-5.0 (Ready)** | Change management proceeds on standard timeline; data work runs in parallel |
| **3.0-3.9 (Needs Work)** | Change management Phase 0 extended by data engineering duration; domain experts engaged early for data work |
| **2.0-2.9 (Significant Gaps)** | Change management paused until data infrastructure matures; use delay to build readiness through listening tours |
| **1.0-1.9 (Blocked)** | Transformation deferred; focus on data infrastructure investment first |

**Adjusted Timeline Formula:**

```
If Data Readiness >= 4.0:
  Time to Value = max(Technical Build, Change Management Phase 1-3)

If Data Readiness 3.0-3.9:
  Time to Value = Data Engineering Time + max(Technical Build, Change Management Phase 1-3)
  Note: Domain experts engaged in Phase 0 for data labeling/validation

If Data Readiness 2.0-2.9:
  Time to Value = Data Infrastructure Time + Data Engineering Time + Technical Build + Change Management
  Note: Use data infrastructure period for Phase 0 listening tours

If Data Readiness < 2.0:
  Defer use case; invest in data infrastructure; reassess in 6 months
```

**Cross-Box Integration Matrix:**

| Box 7 Finding | Box 8 Finding | Combined Action |
|--------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Low resistance | High data readiness | Proceed immediately (Quick Win) |
| Low resistance | Low data readiness | Do data work first; change management ready when data is ready |
| High resistance | High data readiness | Focus change management on adoption; data is not the blocker |
| High resistance | Low data readiness | Both are blockers; consider redesigning use case to reduce both |
| High resistance + identity threat | Requires expert labeling | Highest risk; experts who resist transformation are also needed for data work; use knowledge bounties and Failure Archaeology to engage them |
